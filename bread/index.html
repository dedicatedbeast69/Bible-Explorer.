Words and tips
Baker's percentage
Every ingredient is a percent of the flour. Flour is always 100%. Water at 65% means 65 g of water for every 100 g of flour. This lets you make any size of dough with the same recipe.
Base flour
The flour that makes the 100%. If you use two flours, for example 80% white and 20% whole wheat, together they must make 100%.
Hydration
How much water there is compared to flour. More water gives a softer, stickier dough and bigger holes. 60% is firm. 80% is very wet, like focaccia.
Pre-ferment
A small dough made earlier (a few hours or the day before) and added to the new dough. It gives more flavor and a better crust. Examples: old dough (pate fermentee), poolish, levain.
Bulk rise
The first rise, with all the dough together in one bowl.
Proof
The last rise, after shaping, just before baking.
Fold
Pull one side of the dough up and fold it over the top. Turn the bowl and repeat 4 times. This makes the dough strong without kneading.
Score
Cut the top of the dough with a lame or sharp knife just before baking, so it opens nicely.

Tips

  • Use a digital scale. Put the bowl on, press "tare" (zero), then add the ingredient.
  • Do not put salt and yeast right on top of each other.
  • Warm water (about 80°F / 27°C) makes the dough rise faster. Cold water makes it slower.
  • Bread loses about 10 to 15% of its weight in the oven.
  • Wet dough? Use wet hands, not more flour.
